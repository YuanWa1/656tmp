Description

Three spheres in front of an HDR panorama of a Venice canal, seen from one fixed camera with no geometry. They exist only as a view-space normal map and a per-sphere coverage map (scripts/bake_normal_map.py). Normals are decoded with N = normalize(tex * 2.0 - 1.0). Moving the mouse over the image rotates the environment.

Each pixel's view ray comes from the same camera used for baking. The reflection direction is reflect(D, N), rotated by the environment orientation and mapped to equirectangular coordinates. Lookups pick their mip level from explicit, seam-aware gradients, so the panorama's wrap-around never shows and curved silhouettes do not shimmer as the environment turns.

The silver and gold spheres are polished metals: a mirror reflection weighted by Schlick Fresnel. The white sphere is glazed: Lambert diffuse lit by an irradiance map (the panorama cosine-convolved once on load) plus a Fresnel-weighted reflection that blurs with roughness by sampling coarser mip levels. To make the slider read clearly, the glaze's base reflectance is exaggerated and fades with roughness (0.3 to 0.02; a real glaze is about 0.04). Roughness 0 gives a sharp reflection over white; 1 gives a matte, purely diffuse surface. Panorama: Poly Haven (CC0).
